# The average Saudi university student, by level of study

Prepared for College Helper, 2026-10-02. Evidence tags: **[strong]** means a large or multi-university sample, or official data. **[moderate]** means one university with n > 200. **[weak]** means a small sample, data older than 2020, a secondary source, or anecdote. Bracketed numbers such as [8] refer to the Sources list in section 6.

## 1. Executive summary

- **Scale.** About 1.36–1.57 million students are enrolled in Saudi higher education. The official figures conflict: they are dated 2022, 2023 or 2024 depending on the source [1][2][3]. We found no official breakdown by bachelor's, master's and PhD level after 2006 [5]. All degree-level weighting in this report is therefore qualitative.
- **AI use is close to universal among health-science students, but we have no national usage figure across all fields.** Single-campus studies from 2024–2025 report that 75–98% have used ChatGPT [10][11][12]. A 15-university study (n = 1,390) measured attitudes, not usage rates [8].
- **Students value AI for understanding, and they worry about it.** At King Faisal University, 81.7% said ChatGPT helps them understand concepts, and 65.2% worried that it erodes originality and critical thinking [11]. In a nationwide health-professions survey, 51.73% raised plagiarism concerns [13].
- **AI use by year is inconsistent.** One college found use peaking in year 4 [10]. Another found the most enthusiasm in years 1–2 [11]. Do not hard-code a "more senior means more AI" rule.
- **English is the main barrier in the prep year and early STEM/health years.** Students in English-medium programmes still mostly prefer English as the medium (72.5% in the Saudi subgroup of a 2025 multi-country study) but report that learning is harder [29]. Students favour Arabic–English code-switching for comprehension [30].
- **The prep year acts as a filter.** Pre-university scores predict prep-year GPA most strongly of all outcomes (n = 330,684, 23 universities) [7]. The only national-level attrition data are from before 2012 and show roughly a 65% four-year graduation rate [23].
- **Sleep is poor and studying is phone-centred.** A study at one university (n = 1,153) found that evening smartphone use predicts poor sleep [18]. Most medical students sleep 4–8 hours, and 44% are dissatisfied with their sleep [17].
- **Research skills appear late and unevenly.** Fewer than half of medical students had done research (47.6%, four universities) [27]. Only 39.6% held strongly negative attitudes toward plagiarism [26]. Postgraduate evidence is thin and mostly concerns academic writing [36].

## 3. Level profiles

### 3.1 Preparatory / foundation year (السنة التحضيرية / السنة الأولى المشتركة)

**Profile.** A student just out of school, in their first English-medium or partly English-medium courses. Their grades set their major, and the highest stakes of the degree fall here.

**Key findings**
- Pre-university measures (high-school GPA, GAT, SAAT) predict university performance, and they predict **prep-year GPA most strongly**. The study covered 330,684 undergraduates at 23 universities, 2013–2016 entrants, using ETEC data [7]. **[strong]**
- At King Khalid University (n = 67 pre-clinical medical students), writing was the hardest English skill (27.95%), ahead of reading (25.36%), speaking (24.86%) and listening (12.73%). 56.73% judged the foundation-year English course unsuitable. Yet 85.06% wanted basic sciences taught in English [37]. **[weak]**
- 48.2% of King Saud University prep-year students had high perceived stress at entry, and 45.4% still did after one year (n = 110, 2010–11) [24]. **[weak, dated]**
- Attrition: a 2016 review of 15 Saudi studies found low academic ability and institutional factors (admission, registration, disciplinary rules) were the most common causes. Older studies put attrition at 20–68%, highest in the sciences at 50–68% (1992 data), and four-year graduation at about 65% (sources from 2009–2012) [23]. **[weak, dated]**

**What we don't know:** current national prep-year dismissal or transfer rates; AI use specific to prep-year students; study hours; use of ملخصات and past papers; tutoring.

### 3.2 Early undergraduate (years 1–2, levels 1–4)

**Profile.** Students in mass foundational courses who lean heavily on peers and online video. Most are under GPA pressure on a 5.00 scale.

**Key findings**
- **GPA system.** The standard undergraduate regulation uses a 5.00 scale: ممتاز ≥ 4.50, جيد جداً 3.75 to < 4.50, جيد 2.75 to < 3.75, مقبول 2.00 to < 2.75. Three consecutive academic warnings for a low GPA lead to dismissal [22]. **[strong, official]** We did not verify which institutions use a 4.00 scale.
- **AI.** In Jeddah (n = 420, Mar–May 2024), ChatGPT use was 63.6% in year 1, against 86.7% in year 4 [10]. At KFU (n = 328, Aug–Oct 2025), students in years 1–2 had *higher* perception and attitude scores and used ChatGPT as a scaffold for foundational concepts [11]. **[moderate; the two studies conflict]**
- **Channels.** In a 2016 study (n = 657, 23 medical schools), YouTube was the most-used site (42.3%), and 95.8% said social media helps learning [20]. **[strong, dated]** During COVID, Imam Abdulrahman Bin Faisal University set up official class groups on Telegram [21]. **[weak]**
- **Acceptance drivers.** Across years, intention to use ChatGPT was driven by performance expectancy, social influence (peers using it) and effort expectancy (n = 520, one public university, start of 2023–24) [9]. **[moderate]**

**What we don't know:** how much students use WhatsApp/Telegram course groups and shared ملخصات outside medicine; private tutoring (دروس خصوصية) at university level; use of past papers. None appeared in the peer-reviewed literature we found.

### 3.3 Upper undergraduate (years 3–4+, levels 5–8; 5–6-year programmes)

**Profile.** Specialised, content-heavy coursework. In health programmes, students move into clinical training. Exam anxiety rises, and research first appears as an expectation.

**Key findings**
- Test anxiety among health-professions students at Imam Abdulrahman Bin Faisal University (n = 878, Sep–Nov 2019) was lower in juniors than seniors, though the difference was not significant. Students with high GPAs had significantly lower anxiety [25]. **[moderate]**
- Six universities (n = 675 medical students, Sep–Dec 2019): 87.6% slept 4–8 h, and more than 44% were dissatisfied with their sleep. Pre-clinical and clinical students differed little on lifestyle and study habits [17]. **[strong]**
- Jazan University (n = 1,153, Apr–May 2023): mean PSQI 8.19, which indicates poor sleep. Predictors were evening smartphone use, waking to check the phone, and 1–2 h of phone use at bedtime [18]. **[moderate]**
- KSU healthcare students (pharmacy, nursing, EMS; n = 354, Jan–Apr 2024): 91.2% were familiar with ChatGPT and 75.1% were comfortable using it academically. 66.7% held positive attitudes [12]. In the same paper, 60.2% reportedly saw ChatGPT as promoting cheating; this figure comes from the indexed results, not the abstract [12]. **[moderate]**
- KFU medicine: 73.8% used ChatGPT to summarise research articles [11]. **[moderate]**

**What we don't know:** findings outside health sciences, for example engineering, business or humanities by year; how much students cram before exams, measured directly.

### 3.4 Final year: graduation project, co-op, internship year (سنة الامتياز)

**Profile.** Students doing applied work under heavy evaluation. Health interns juggle shifts, SMLE preparation and residency matching. Students in other majors handle a capstone or co-op placement.

**Key findings**
- Umm Al-Qura University medicine (n = 337): 87.2% had done at least one research project, and sixth-year students had the highest participation. Barriers were poor academic teaching (39.5%), lack of active research groups (38.6%) and lack of time (36.2%) [28]. **[moderate]**
- Four universities (n = 435, Dec 2021–Apr 2022): only 47.6% had taken part in research. The top motivator was residency admission (44.8%). The top barriers were lack of time (29.2%) and lack of mentoring (16.8%) [27]. **[strong]**
- Medical interns in Dammam and Al-Khobar (n = 117, Oct 2024–Apr 2025): overall burnout 7.7%, but 55.6% had low personal accomplishment and 42.7% high depersonalisation. Anxiety was 37.6% and depression 28.2% [32]. **[weak]**
- A 2025 narrative review describes the internship year as marked by heavy workload, long hours, SMLE preparation and adapting to new teams [34]. **[weak, no primary data]**
- Co-op: a review notes weak university–employer partnerships and employers who are unprepared to host trainees or assign meaningful projects [35]. **[weak]**

**What we don't know:** anything quantitative on non-health graduation projects (topic selection, coding, report writing), co-op experience by major, or AI use during the internship year.

### 3.5 Postgraduate (master's and PhD)

**Profile.** A small population whose size is not known from current data. Students write theses in English or Arabic and frequently work with limited supervision.

**Key findings**
- Saudi graduate students' biggest academic-writing problems were presenting complex information in clear sentences and building strong arguments [36]. **[weak, sample not retrieved]**
- Studies of Saudi postgraduates (largely those studying in the UK) report struggles with academic vocabulary, avoiding plagiarism, cohesion and critical argument, linked to little prior EAP training [38]. **[weak]**
- The 2016 attrition review notes that Saudi universities lack academic databases and thesis repositories, which limits literature access [23]. **[weak, dated]**

**What we don't know:** generative-AI adoption among Saudi postgraduates (we found no Saudi-specific study); time to degree; the share of theses written in Arabic versus English; supervision ratios.

### 3.6 National AI guidance (applies to all levels)

- SDAIA issued *Generative AI in Education Guidelines* in November 2023, covering transparency, accountability, privacy, human oversight and risk management [14]. **[strong, official]**
- SDAIA launched a national cross-disciplinary Data and AI curriculum that is to become mandatory for all undergraduates, with MoUs signed with 14 universities [15]. **[moderate, press report]**
- Public generative-AI policies from individual universities (for example KSU or KAU) could not be located during this research. The 15-university study calls for use policies and misuse detection [8]. **Gap.**

## 5. Implications for College Helper

Each implication lists the sources it rests on, so the team can see how firm it is.

**Prep year**
1. **Bilingual by default.** Explain in Arabic, then give the English term in parentheses. Offer a "say it again in English" toggle. *Rests on [29][30][37]: strong + weak.*
2. **Coach writing first.** For English tasks, start with sentence- and paragraph-level writing feedback, the skill rated hardest [37]. *Weak; validate with our own users.*
3. **Be GPA-aware and calm.** Treat the prep year as high-stakes, because prep GPA decides the major [7]. Default to exam-oriented study plans with a reassuring tone [24]. *Strong + weak.*

**Years 1–2**
4. **Understanding before answers.** Students already use AI as a concept scaffold [11] and adopt it because peers do [9]. Lead with explanation, then quiz the student before showing a full solution to graded-looking problems. *Moderate.*
5. **Phone-first, short replies.** Send short, chunked answers that read well on a phone. A late-night mode can nudge students toward sleep near exams. *Rests on [18]: moderate.* Our phone-versus-laptop share for studying is unmeasured.
6. **Video-friendly.** When students mention YouTube lectures, help them turn the video into notes and practice questions [20]. *Strong but dated (2016).*

**Years 3–4+ (and 5–6-year programmes)**
7. **Make integrity visible.** About half to two-thirds of students worry about plagiarism or originality [11][12][13]. Mark which parts of a reply the student may paraphrase and which need a citation, and refuse to ghost-write graded work. *Moderate–strong.*
8. **Support paper summaries with verification.** 73.8% summarise papers with ChatGPT [11]. Always link back to the source, flag uncertainty, and push the student to read the methods section. *Moderate.*
9. **Exam-anxiety support.** Offer spaced-retrieval and past-question practice. The link between high GPA and lower anxiety suggests mastery tools are worth building [25]. *Moderate.* The specific value of past papers and ملخصات is unmeasured, so test it.

**Final year / internship**
10. **Research mentor mode.** The main barriers are lack of mentoring, time and research groups [27][28]. Provide project scoping, a methods checklist and a citation manager walkthrough. *Strong + moderate.*
11. **Respect the intern's time.** Give terse, high-yield SMLE-style practice and quick clinical concept refreshers. Use no long Socratic chains here. *Rests on [32][34]: weak.*
12. **Co-op and capstone help.** Report structuring and workplace-communication templates. *Weak [35]; assumption-led, so validate it.*

**Postgraduate**
13. **Argument and cohesion coaching.** Offer outline-to-argument feedback, academic vocabulary help and paraphrase-with-citation drills [36][38]. *Weak.*
14. **Default to strict integrity.** Never draft thesis text wholesale. Explain how to disclose AI use in line with the SDAIA principles of transparency and human oversight [14]. *Strong (official).*
15. **Discovery help.** Teach literature-search strategy, since limited database and repository access is reported [23]. *Weak, dated.*

**All levels:** Adaptation by level is only as good as our level detection. Ask for level and major at onboarding, because evidence on AI use by year conflicts [10][11].
